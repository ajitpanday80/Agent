## Steps
1. Ask exactly what happened: did they only see the pop-up, or did they also call a number, let anyone control the computer, enter passwords, or pay anything?
2. If they only saw the pop-up: tell them not to call the number or click anything in it. Real security warnings from tech companies never ask you to call a phone number.
3. Ask them to close the browser. If the mouse does not work, press Alt and F4 together on Windows. If the browser will not close, restart the computer. Then create a normal low-priority ticket so a technician can scan the machine.
4. If they called a number, allowed remote access, entered passwords or paid: this is a security incident. Create the ticket with security concern set to true and follow the instructions the system returns.
5. Tell them to hang up on the scammer, not to give anything more, and that a technician will call right away.
6. If they paid by card or bank transfer, they should call their card company or bank immediately. If they paid with gift cards, they should contact the card issuer quickly.

## Escalate if
They gave remote access, passwords, or any payment. Anyone asking for payment by gift card, wire transfer or Bitcoin is a scammer.

## Technician notes
If remote access was given, disconnect the machine from the network without powering it off, remove remote-access tools, scan, and reset credentials for accounts used on that machine. Reporting: FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov; Microsoft at microsoft.com/reportascam for Microsoft-branded scams.
